Introduction

Modern architecture with Filipino cultural identity.

Leandro V. Locsin was one of the most influential Filipino architects of the 20th century. His work combined modern architecture with Filipino cultural identity through monumental structures, massive concrete forms, floating volumes, open spaces, geometric shapes, and dramatic light-shadow relationships.

His projects include important cultural, religious, civic, and institutional buildings such as the Cultural Center of the Philippines, Philippine International Convention Center, Chapel of the Holy Sacrifice, Folk Arts Theater, and the original Manila International Airport Terminal 1.

His work established a distinctive form of Filipino modernism by translating openness, large roofs, shade, ventilation, and environmental connection into modern forms rather than simply copying traditional architecture.

Selection Rationale

Why Leandro V. Locsin?

Locsin matters because he demonstrated that modern materials and advanced engineering could communicate Filipino culture rather than erase it.

Locsin shaped modern Philippine architecture by showing that modernism could express Filipino culture and national identity. He combined modern materials and advanced engineering with principles derived from traditional Filipino and Southeast Asian architecture.

Instead of directly copying vernacular styles, he reinterpreted open plans, sweeping roofs, extended eaves, elevated platforms, natural ventilation, and shaded spaces into a modern architectural language. He also demonstrated architecture's capacity to operate as monumental civic art that communicates national aspirations, cultural pride, and historical continuity.

Fun Facts

Did you know?

Eight notable highlights from Locsin's life, artistic roots, and architectural career.

Pianist before architect

Locsin initially studied piano performance at the UST Conservatory of Music before switching to architecture.

The world's largest residence

He designed the Istana Nurul Iman in Brunei, recorded as the world's largest residential state palace.

Master of the CCP Complex

Locsin designed nearly all original major structures within the CCP Complex, including the CCP Main Building, Folk Arts Theater, PICC, and the Philippine Plaza Hotel.

A pioneer at age 27

He completed the thin-shell concrete Chapel of the Holy Sacrifice when he was only 27 years old.

Collaborator with National Artists

He frequently integrated fine art into his buildings, working directly with Vicente Manansala, Arturo Luz, Napoleon Abueva, and Ang Kiukok.

Inspired by the salakot

He modeled the roof structure of the Immaculate Heart of Mary Parish after the traditional Filipino salakot hat.

The best-known part of the CCP

The CCP complex includes several buildings, and the main theater is the best-known part of his design.

An example of regional modernism

His architecture is often taught as an example of regional modernism, meaning modern design adapted to local climate and culture.
