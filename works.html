Famous Work · 01

Chapel of the Holy Sacrifice

Location
UP Diliman, Quezon City
Year
1955
Type
Religious Architecture

Stylistic Evolution: Represents Locsin's early breakthrough, showing his ability to innovate sacred space through modern structural engineering. A circular church covered by a thin concrete dome, it is widely regarded as a pioneering modernist church in the Philippines.

Elements of Art
  • Line & Form: Defined by a sweeping three-dimensional parabolic dome, the first thin-shell concrete dome constructed in the Philippines. Continuous curved lines create a clear dome of space unobstructed by interior load-bearing columns.
  • Space & Light: The open-air perimeter lacks exterior walls, permitting continuous cross-ventilation and natural daylight that connects the central altar to the surrounding natural environment.
Principles of Design
  • Radial Balance & Unity: The circular footprint places the central altar at the focal center, drawing visual attention inward from every angle.
  • Integration of Visual Arts: Structure and art work together through embedded works by fellow National Artists: terrazzo floor designs by Arturo Luz, murals of the Stations of the Cross by Vicente Manansala and Ang Kiukok, and a carved double-sided crucifix by Napoleon Abueva.

Famous Work · 02

Cultural Center of the Philippines (CCP Main Building / Tanghalang Pambansa)

Location
CCP Complex, Pasay City
Construction
1966–1969
Type
Performing Arts Center

Stylistic Evolution: Marks the peak of Locsin's brutalist period and establishes his iconic floating volume technique on a national scale. His most famous project, the main theater building appears to hover over a recessed base and faces Manila Bay.

Elements of Art
  • Mass & Texture: Dominated by an upper rectangular volume built from travertine aggregate concrete. The raw, textured surface emphasizes monumental solidity.
  • Value (Chiaroscuro): Deeply recessed glass curtain walls underneath cast intense shadows, visually severing the upper block from its lower support structure.
Principles of Design
  • Contrast & Asymmetrical Balance: A massive 8-story concrete volume is suspended 12 meters outward over a glass podium and reflecting pools, setting structural weight against a floating effect.
  • Scale & Proportion: Designed on a monumental public scale, with an expansive podium drive that lifts the building above the reclaimed coastal landscape.

Famous Work · 03

Philippine International Convention Center (PICC)

Location
CCP Complex, Pasay City
Opened
September 5, 1976
Type
Civic & Convention Center

Stylistic Evolution: Shows Locsin's mastery of modular civic architecture, designed to manage international crowds while keeping spatial elegance. It is a major venue for state events and international meetings.

Elements of Art
  • Line & Form: Crisp horizontal lines, broad geometric cantilevers, and board-formed concrete surfaces that keep a wood-grain texture.
  • Space: Expansive multi-level foyers, open plazas, and courtyard gardens give clear circulation across five distinct building modules.
Principles of Design
  • Rhythm & Repetition: Modular concrete frames and cantilevered overhangs repeat across the façade, creating a structural visual rhythm.
  • Proportion & Structural Honesty: The exterior volumes correspond directly to the interior hall spaces, without decorative ornament.

Famous Work · 04

Ninoy Aquino International Airport Terminal 1 (NAIA Terminal 1)

Location
Parañaque City
Completed
1981
Type
Transit Infrastructure

Stylistic Evolution: Applies Locsin's tropical modernist principles to functional transit infrastructure, adapting international transportation standards to local climate demands.

Elements of Art
  • Line & Geometry: Clean horizontal lines and rectangular window geometries organize the visual flow of a high-traffic terminal.
  • Texture & Material: Smooth structural concrete is combined with warm wooden paneling inside, balancing durability with tactile warmth.
Principles of Design
  • Rhythm & Flow: Repeating structural bays guide passenger movement logically through arrival, departure, and baggage circulation zones.
  • Emphasis & Functionality: Deep exterior overhangs emphasize shade and rain protection, adapting modern international airport standards to tropical rainfall.

Famous Work · 05

Istana Nurul Iman

Location
Bandar Seri Begawan, Brunei
Completed
1984
Type
Royal Palace & State Residence

Stylistic Evolution: Represents Locsin's international expansion, adapting Southeast Asian vernacular motifs into the sultan's palace, one of the largest residential palaces in the world and his biggest overseas commission.

Elements of Art
  • Form & Texture: Merges monumental concrete framing with traditional Islamic geometric motifs, gilded domes, and dramatic sloped roof surfaces.
  • Space & Scale: Covers over 2.2 million square feet, organizing grand banquet halls, royal courtyards, and state quarters into a unified palace complex.
Principles of Design
  • Unity & Variety: Blends modern structural brutalism with traditional Islamic and Malay architectural forms.
  • Hierarchy & Dominance: The central gold-leaf dome is the primary focal point, anchoring the surrounding administrative and residential wings.

International Case Study

The Philippine Pavilion at Expo '70

An expressionist shell interpretation representing modern Philippine architecture on the international stage.

Locsin's impact extended beyond buildings in the Philippines. In 1970 he designed the Philippine Pavilion at Expo '70 in Osaka, Japan.

The pavilion showed how Philippine architecture could be represented internationally through modern forms rather than through a literal reproduction of traditional buildings. The National Commission for Culture and the Arts referred to Locsin's pavilion as an expressionist interpretation of a shell.
