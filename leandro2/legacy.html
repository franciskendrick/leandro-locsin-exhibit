Leandro Locsin's legacy

Leandro V. Locsin left a lasting mark on the built environment of the Philippines. He showed that Philippine architecture could be modern without imitating foreign models, and that modern tropical architecture can stay deeply connected to national identity while meeting contemporary global standards.

In 1990, Locsin was proclaimed National Artist of the Philippines for Architecture, honoring his lasting impact on design and culture. His firm, Leandro V. Locsin and Partners, continued his approach after his death on November 15, 1994. His landmarks are still used, studied, and debated, especially regarding preservation, since several have been renovated or placed at risk over the years.

His structural language can be summarized through key concepts:

  • Mass
  • Space
  • Light
  • Shadow
  • Form
  • Culture
  • Identity

Architecture and nation-building

During the 1960s and 1970s, the Philippines invested heavily in cultural and convention buildings. Locsin designed many of them, so his work is closely tied to the era's ideas of national identity. This also invites discussion, since many of these projects were linked to the Marcos administration's cultural program.

Locsin and heritage conservation

Locsin's buildings have become part of the country's architectural heritage, but this has also raised questions about preservation.

Several of his buildings have been modified, pulled down, renovated, or adapted for new purposes. This shows that architectural heritage is not merely about remembering well-known architects. It also requires decisions about how historic buildings can be kept while meeting the demands of modern times.

The Philippine International Convention Center is an example of conservation in practice. In 2022, the PICC and several permanently installed artworks were declared National Cultural Treasures. The more recent rehabilitation has tried to modernize the facility while keeping intact the important elements of Locsin's original architectural character.
